🛴 E-Scooter Smart Wheel Analyzer

Enter the wheel radius and motor speed to analyze the rotational motion of a scooter tire.

How to use this analyzer

  1. Type the wheel radius r (choose m, cm or in) and the motor speed N in RPM. Optionally change the time t.
  2. Read the results in 2 · Results. Every value is shown in SI units (rad/s, m/s, m/s², s, rad, m) and updates as you type.
  3. Watch the wheel in 3. The green arrow is the velocity of point P, the orange arrow is its centripetal acceleration. Use the slider to slow it down.
  4. Check the θ–t graph, then solve ω, vt and ac by hand in the Test panel (7) and press Check answers.
  5. Open 9 · Validation evidence to see three input sets compared with manual calculations.

Valid ranges: r = 0.01–1 m, N = 1–3000 RPM, t = 0–3600 s. Invalid or empty entries show a red message and no result.

6 · Real-life application

Electric scooters, e-bikes and cars use the same relations. The motor tells us how fast the wheel spins (RPM → ω). Multiplying by the wheel radius gives the scooter's ground speed (vt = ωr), which is what the speedometer shows. Engineers also check the centripetal acceleration (ac = ω²r) because it is the inward acceleration a point on the tire needs to stay on its circular path; the larger it is, the more stress the tire, rim and spokes must withstand.

8 · Model assumptions

  • Uniform circular motion: the wheel turns at a constant angular velocity ω, so angular acceleration α = 0 and tangential acceleration at = rα = 0. The only acceleration of point P is the centripetal acceleration ac, directed toward the center.
  • Rigid wheel, fixed radius: r is measured from the axle to the tire edge and does not change (no tire deformation).
  • Rolling without slipping: the scooter's ground speed equals the tangential speed of the rim, vCM = rω. Slipping or skidding is not modeled.
  • Direct drive: N is the rotation rate of the wheel itself (no gearbox is modeled). Air resistance and friction are ignored.
  • Equal wheels: the front and rear wheels have the same radius, so both turn at the same ω. The analysis (point P and the arrows) is drawn on the rear motor wheel.
  • Angles in radians: 1 revolution = 2π rad; θ(rad) = π/180 × θ(deg). 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h, and g = 9.81 m/s² is used only for the "≈ g" comparison.
